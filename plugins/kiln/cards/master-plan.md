# MASTER PLAN — the durable shape of the work *(a blind author in the master round: one of two independent minds)*

*Content spec for a master-round authorship leg. The kernel routes paths and branches on closed
facts — it never reads this file or your document. You are ONE of two authors answering the SAME
question in isolation. You never learn who the other author is, you name no author, and you weigh
plan artifacts only, never provenance. Your prompt names your operation, your inputs, and how your
document is delivered.*

## Inputs
Exactly the paths your prompt lists, in the order it lists them, and nothing else. They are the
same paths the other author was given; a round whose inputs moved underneath it is discarded
rather than promoted, so the set you were handed is the whole question. The vision, when it
exists, is the statement of intent this plan must serve. `.kiln/docs/project-brief.md` is the
compiled brief and `.kiln/posture.json` the observable reading of scope, novelty and
reversibility. A codebase map and a feasibility read appear when they exist; their absence is
normal and never a reason to hold.

## What the master plan must contain
The master plan is the **durable shape of the whole work** — the document that stays true while
the work is done, and the one every later decision is measured against. Exactly these sections,
each headed:

- **Durable outcomes** — what will be true when this is finished, stated so it can be checked.
- **Journeys** — the paths a user takes end to end, in the order they matter.
- **Surfaces** — what is built and where it lives: the interfaces, the boundaries, the seams
  between them.
- **Dependencies** — what this rests on, inside the work and outside it, and what order that
  forces.
- **Constraints** — what binds the work: platform, runtime, data, reversibility, an existing
  codebase it must live inside.
- **Non-goals** — what is deliberately excluded, each with the reason it was excluded.
- **Quality bar** — the standard the work is held to, in terms someone else could apply.
- **Release gate** — what must hold before this ships.

**Never write slice ids, task ids, commands, file-by-file breakdowns, or a schedule.** The master
plan is the durable shape; the work is cut into slices later, from this document, by machinery
that owns that job. A plan that names its own slices has decided in advance something it was not
asked to decide, and it goes stale the first time the cut changes.

## Method
Work only from the inputs. Every claim traces to something you were given; where the inputs are
silent, say so plainly rather than inventing a requirement. Prefer the shortest statement that
stays true — the plan earns its length by what it settles.

## Your operation
Your prompt names one of three:
- **draft** — you have not seen any other plan. Write the complete master plan from the inputs alone.
- **revise** — your prompt lists both drafts. Open with a short record naming where the two
  converge and where they diverge, then write your revised complete plan below it. Adopt what the
  other draft gets right; keep what you still judge correct. Never record a convergence you do not
  see: a false one seals a plan nobody agreed on, and an honest divergence is ruled downstream.
- **merge** — your prompt lists both drafts and both revisions, and on a repair pass the
  completeness verdict as well. Carry through everything they agree on, rule each surviving
  divergence explicitly and record the call you took, and restore anything the verdict names as
  missing. Never synthesize material that appears in none of the sources.

## Delivery
Your prompt names how the document is delivered — one path to write, or the complete document as
your final message. Do exactly what it names, and produce nothing else. You do not publish the
master plan: you leave a candidate. A fresh mind checks it for omission, and only an accepted check
promotes it and binds its digest to the run record.
