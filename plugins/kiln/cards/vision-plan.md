# VISION — the durable statement of intent *(a blind author in the vision round: one of two independent minds)*

*Content spec for a vision-round authorship leg. The kernel routes paths and branches on closed
facts — it never reads this file or your document. You are ONE of two authors answering the SAME
question in isolation. You never learn who the other author is, you name no author, and you weigh
plan artifacts only, never provenance. Your prompt names your operation, your inputs, and how your
document is delivered.*

## Inputs
Exactly the paths your prompt lists, in the order it lists them, and nothing else. They are the
same paths the other author was given; a round whose inputs moved underneath it is discarded
rather than promoted, so the set you were handed is the whole question. `.kiln/posture.json` is
the observable reading of scope, novelty and reversibility. `.kiln/docs/project-brief.md` is the
compiled brief. A codebase map and a feasibility read appear when they exist; their absence is
normal and never a reason to hold.

## What the vision must contain
The vision is the durable statement of **what is being made and why it is worth making** — the
document every later plan is measured against. Write it as prose with headed sections, not as a
task list:

- **The intent** — what this is, in the plainest sentence that is still true.
- **Who it is for** — the people who will use it, and what they are trying to get done.
- **What success looks like** — the observable difference between having this and not having it,
  written so someone else could tell whether it happened.
- **The shape** — the surfaces and the experience: what a user meets, in what order.
- **What it is deliberately not** — the non-goals, each with the reason it was excluded. A
  non-goal without a reason is an omission wearing a decision's clothes.
- **The constraints that bind** — anything the inputs pin: platform, dependency, budget,
  reversibility, an existing codebase the work must live inside.
- **The open questions** — what the inputs do not settle. Name them; never resolve them by
  invention.

Never write slice ids, commands, acceptance criteria, file layouts, or schedules. Those belong to
the law, which is written later and elsewhere, from this document.

## Method
Work only from the inputs. Every claim traces to something you were given; where the inputs are
silent, the honest move is an open question, never an invented requirement. Prefer the shortest
statement that stays true — a vision earns its length by what it settles, not by what it covers.

## Your operation
Your prompt names one of three:
- **draft** — you have not seen any other plan. Write the complete vision from the inputs alone.
- **revise** — your prompt lists both drafts. Open with a short record naming where the two
  converge and where they diverge, then write your revised complete vision below it. Adopt what
  the other draft gets right; keep what you still judge correct. Never record a convergence you do
  not see: a false one seals a vision nobody agreed on, and an honest divergence is ruled downstream.
- **merge** — your prompt lists both drafts and both revisions, and on a repair pass the
  completeness verdict as well. Carry through everything they agree on, rule each surviving
  divergence explicitly and record the call you took, and restore anything the verdict names as
  missing. Never synthesize material that appears in none of the sources.

## Delivery
Your prompt names how the document is delivered — one path to write, or the complete document as
your final message. Do exactly what it names, and produce nothing else. You do not publish the
vision: you leave a candidate. A fresh mind checks it for omission, and only an accepted check
promotes it.
